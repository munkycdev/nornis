# Session 3 — The widow and the wood

From the Sunken Ferry east to Thornhollow, on the fringe of the Mistwood.
Half a day's walk; Tam Rush went home to Windrow with strict instructions and
obvious intentions to ignore them.

- Thornhollow: hedge-and-timber village, older than it looks. Widow Harrach
  keeps the last real memory of the river country in a house full of rubbings
  and knotted cords. She took one look at the bronze fitting and went quiet
  in a way none of us liked.
- The bell-in-waves mark is Greyhollow work, she says — the abbey's foundry
  stamp. And the fitting is a hanging mount: "You do not cast that unless you
  mean to hang something the size of a cart." She called the thing it hung the
  Vesper Bell, and then poured her tea out the window, which she would not
  explain.
- From her rubbings: Greyhollow Abbey did not simply fall into ruin. Something
  happened there about forty years ago — the abbey's own carts came down to
  the crossing at night, and after that the ferry was "let drown." The
  Bellwardens (a lay order attached to the abbey — new name to us) disappear
  from every record the same season.
- Crossing the Mistwood to reach the abbey: the wood earns its name. Fog with
  a direction to it. Shrike-stones along the old pilgrim path — waymarkers,
  Harrach says, and warnings.
- Ambush at the ford inside the wood: four hired blades, not Groundlings —
  cleaner, quieter, paid better. They knew our route. Kestrel put two down
  and the others ran. On one of them: good silver, and a letter in a fine
  hand instructing "delay them short of the abbey" — signed only with a
  bell-and-wave sigil. The Salt Factor's hand, we assume.
- Camped inside the tree line. Elowen dreamed of a bell tolling under water
  and woke with her hair wet. Nobody has a comfortable explanation and she
  has stopped offering uncomfortable ones.

Next: Greyhollow Abbey itself, and whatever the monks drowned rather than
keep.
